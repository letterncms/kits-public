The path leaves the harbour behind the fish auction and climbs straight into the dunes. In summer it is busy until the first gate; in November you will have it to yourself by the time you can see the sea.

::: image {"id":"cp-dunes","props":{"alt":"A sandy path between wooden fences through the dunes.","image":["01m7ba38tt2x1srp5cr5s8my7t"],"caption":"The first kilometre, between the old sand fences."}}
:::

The sand is firm after rain and soft as flour after a dry week. Wear something you can walk in for three hours, and bring a flask: the café at the lighthouse closes for the winter on the first of November.

::: image {"id":"cp-bay","props":{"alt":"A wide bay with surf rolling onto a sandy beach below the dunes.","image":["01m7ba38twytx4z8ey0ypttrch"],"caption":"Halfway: the bay below the watchtower, at high water."}}
:::

## The way back

Come back along the beach when the tide is out. The groynes are black with mussels and you can walk on the wet sand all the way to the harbour wall, with the light going pink behind you.

::: image {"id":"cp-groynes","props":{"alt":"Someone walking along old wooden groynes on a pebble beach.","image":["01m7ba38tvwqtn00wkdqtwmt3e"],"caption":"The groynes below the harbour, at low water."}}
:::
