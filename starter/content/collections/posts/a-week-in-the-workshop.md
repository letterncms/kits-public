Monday starts in the yard. The oak for this table has been drying under the lean-to for eighteen months, and the first job is choosing which boards go where: the quiet grain for the top, the wilder pieces for the legs, where nobody looks closely.

:::

## Tuesday and Wednesday

Everything is cut a little too big and left overnight, so the wood can move before we take it to size. Then the joints: twenty-four mortises and tenons, cut by hand, each one fitted to its own partner and numbered in pencil.

> Machines are good at the same thing many times. A table is the same thing four times, which is not quite enough.

## Thursday

Glue-up is the only part of the week that is never quiet. Two people, a dozen clamps and about eleven minutes before the glue starts to set.

## Friday

The top goes on, the oil goes on, and the table stands in the middle of the workshop for the rest of the day while everyone walks past it, touching the edge. On Monday it goes to its new home, and the next stack of boards comes in from the yard.
