An echo sounder hears the top of the silt. A lead line feels the bottom of it. In a harbour that is dredged by the centimetre, that difference is the whole job.

So every sounding run ends the way it did in 1850: seven kilos of lead, a marked line, and someone calling the depth over their shoulder. We write it in the margin of the sheet, in pencil.
