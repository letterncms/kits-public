## Before we go out

Every commission starts with one question: what moves, and how fast? A sandbank that walks a metre a week needs different instruments, and a different sheet, than a crowd that turns over every ninety seconds. We agree on the scale, the datum and the day the sheet has to be true.

## On site

We measure with whatever the ground allows. A lead line still beats an echo sounder in a metre of silt. Every instrument is checked against a second one, and every reading is logged twice: by the logger, and by a person with a pencil.

## At the table

Readings become isolines by rule, then someone corrects them by eye. Two people sign each sheet: the one who measured and the one who drew.

## Afterwards

A printed sheet carries its date and a correction schedule. A live layer follows the instruments we leave behind, for as long as you keep them running.
