Between the ebb and the flood the entrance at Zeebrugge goes quiet for about forty minutes. Pilots call it the slack hour and plan every deep-draught arrival around it. The trouble is that it does not arrive everywhere at once.

We moored eleven current meters across the outer harbour for two spring-neap cycles and drew what they said: thirteen sheets, one for each half hour either side of high water, with the still water printed as blank paper.

## What we measured

- Current speed and set at eleven stations, every ten minutes for 29 days
- Depth across the entrance, re-sounded after each storm
- Wind at the mole head, for the days the tide ran late

The atlas is now carried on the pilot boats. Its correction layer is fed by three of the meters we left behind.
